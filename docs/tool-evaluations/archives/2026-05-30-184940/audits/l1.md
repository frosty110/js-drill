## Strengths
- **Misses feed three independent signal lanes plus a cross-lesson lane** (`12a-l1.js:369, 405, 301-309, 514`): `state.weakness` (lesson-grain), `state.misses` (concept-grain via tag chips), `state.history` (event log), AND carry-over writes back to the *source* lesson's weakness — first surface in the app where re-checking an old gap from a new context actually erodes the gap.
- **Per-session shuffle resists pattern memorization** (`12a-l1.js:230-238`) — question order AND option positions both reshuffle on retry; cached for tab-switch survival via `_cacheGet/_cacheSet`.
- **Carry-over card opens cross-lesson interleave inside the per-lesson tab** (`12a-l1.js:197-211, 253-267`) — without breaking the per-lesson pass criterion; the card is rendered and persisted separately from `localState.perQ`.

## Weaknesses
- **Recognition ceiling on Encoding (1/3)** — by construction L1 can never produce free recall. This is the design intent (PROFILE.md:30 "L1 (multiple choice) is the smoothest interaction on mobile"), but it means L1 alone can't move Encoding past 1/3. The salvage is layering production-direction recall on top, not replacing the MC core.
- **Feedback rarely explains the user's specific wrong pick** (`12a-l1.js:379`) — `q.explain` is authored *for the question* (why the correct answer is correct), not per-option. The "Ask AI to teach me" export (`:471`) does diagnose specific wrong picks but requires manual action.
- **Carry-over is one card per session** (`12a-l1.js:255-267`) — if there are 5 weak-spot lessons accumulating, only 1 surfaces per L1 session. Rapid-Fire L1 is the higher-throughput cross-lesson tool; the L1 tab carry-over is the leak-into-mainstream pass.

## Salvage path (if IMPROVE)

L1 reached the KEEP, ship-quality band at 18/21. Remaining headroom is Encoding (capped at 1/3 by the recognition format) and Transfer-context (capped at ~2/3 for any MC surface). A defensible single edit if the band needs to be pushed to 19:
