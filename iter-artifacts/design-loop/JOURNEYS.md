# Key Journeys / Jobs-To-Be-Done

The design must make these effortless. Each is written as a job ("when… I want…
so I can…") with the success bar. If a redesign makes any of these *slower or
harder*, it has failed regardless of how it looks.

## J1 · Start a session (the core job, ~80% of use)
**When** I have 20 free minutes on my phone, **I want** to be drilling the right
thing immediately, **so I can** make progress without deciding.
- **Success:** app open → first rep on screen in **≤1 tap**, ≤3 seconds. The app
  chose (due > weak > path) and biased by recent diagnostic signal. Override is
  one tap away but unnecessary.
- **Anti-fail:** a landing screen that asks me to choose a mode first.

## J2 · Do a rep (L1 tap / L2 short-type)
**When** I'm drilling, **I want** a clean, focused question with a satisfying
grade, **so I can** stay in flow across many quick reps.
- **Success:** one question, big tap targets, instant feedback + terse why,
  one-tap advance. 30-second cycles. Streak/progress ambient.
- **Anti-fail:** cramped targets, cluttered chrome, feedback I have to hunt for.

## J3 · Resume after interruption
**When** my bus arrives mid-session, **I want** to leave and come back exactly
where I was, **so I can** never lose momentum.
- **Success:** state persists instantly; reopening lands on the same rep/scroll.

## J4 · See if I'm improving (at a glance)
**When** I finish or open the app, **I want** to feel my progress, **so I can**
stay motivated and know I'm interview-ready.
- **Success:** ambient streak + today-vs-yesterday + readiness/gap chip on the
  surface I'm already on. A deeper Progress surface exists but I rarely need it.
- **Anti-fail:** progress buried behind navigation; gamification that hides real readiness.

## J5 · Browse to a specific thing
**When** I want a particular lesson/section/pattern, **I want** to find it fast,
**so I can** target a known gap.
- **Success:** browse/search/filter as a first-class surface; ≤2 taps to any lesson; palette for power users.

## J6 · Reach a power mode (the long tail)
**When** I want a specific drill (Recognize, Rapid-Fire, Swap-Bench, Mock, a cram
tool…), **I want** to get to it without hunting four menus, **so I can** use the
right tool for today.
- **Success:** a contextual practice launcher surfaces the relevant few; the
  command palette (`⌘K`) reaches *any* mode by name; deep-links `#/m/<mode>` still work.

## J7 · Adjust the app (settings)
**When** I want to change text size / haptics / audio / back up my data, **I
want** one obvious place, **so I can** do it without spelunking.
- **Success:** one grouped Settings surface (Display · Feedback · Data & sync · Install & offline).

## J8 · First run (new user)
**When** I open the app the first time, **I want** to understand it and start in
seconds, **so I can** get value before deciding to invest.
- **Success:** a designed first-run that gets me to a first rep fast; not a wall of features.

## J9 · Cram before an interview
**When** my interview is in days, **I want** a curated path + the crux of each
pattern + a readiness read, **so I can** focus my limited time.
- **Success:** clear cram/plan surface; "key ideas" crux views; readiness signal; zero busywork.

---

**Priority order for the redesign:** J1, J2, J3, J4 are the beating heart (the
commuter loop) — nail these first. J5–J9 must be excellent but are reached less
often; they can be progressively disclosed.
