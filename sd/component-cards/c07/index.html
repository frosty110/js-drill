Control & Protection

Keeping load inside what the system can survive. 6 building blocks drilled by recall: the signal that says reach for one, what it costs, how it breaks, which sibling rules it out, and what it is doing across the canonical problems.

Component Flash Cards 30 questions

Results carried by this link

Key ideas

  • Rate Limiting — Capping how many requests a caller may make in a window, to protect capacity and enforce fairness.
  • Backpressure — Letting a saturated consumer slow its producer, so queues stay bounded instead of absorbing an overload until something breaks.
  • Circuit Breaker — Tripping a dependency call to fail fast after repeated failures, then probing periodically to see whether it has recovered.
  • Retry with Backoff & Jitter — Re-attempting a failed call on an exponentially increasing, randomised delay, so transient failures heal without synchronising the fleet.
  • Load Shedding — Deliberately dropping or degrading lower-priority work under overload so the system serves its most important traffic instead of failing at everything.
  • Bulkhead Isolation — Partitioning resources — thread pools, connections, even fleets — so one dependency or tenant cannot consume everything.
