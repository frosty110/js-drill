Audit Log & Provenance

An append-only record of what was decided and what it was shown, pinned to the versions in effect, so the decision can be reconstructed later.

Governance & Accountability signature mechanism 6 problems

Reach for it when

  • A decision has to be defended to someone external — a regulator, an auditor, a counterparty — months or years after it was made
  • The inputs came from somewhere you do not control and will not replay: a third-party API, a vendor feed, a model
  • Money moved, or access was granted or refused, so 'what did we do and on what basis' is a question that will eventually be asked

Don't reach for it when

  • The decision is trivially re-derivable from durable inputs you already retain — then the log stores no information you did not have
  • You only want debugging visibility, where tracing and structured logs are cheaper and are built for the job

What it costs you

  • The record outlives the schema that wrote it, so it must be self-describing and denormalized rather than a web of foreign keys
  • Retention is measured in years and is usually a legal floor rather than a tuning knob
  • Evidence has to be captured at decision time, which means storing the third-party response rather than the query that produced it
  • Write amplification on every step of every decision, which is the price of the record being complete rather than sampled

How it breaks

  • Recording the conclusion but not what was shown, so the log asserts a decision it cannot evidence
  • Pinning the code version but not the policy, prompt or data snapshot — any one of them changing changes the answer
  • A log the application can update in place, which records the current story rather than what happened
  • Citing a live source instead of a stored snapshot, so the citation resolves to whatever that source says today
  • Promising deterministic replay of a non-deterministic process; preserved inputs and a comparison is what you can honestly offer

Instead, consider

  • Transactional Outbox / CDC — Captures every state change for downstream consumers; an audit log captures the decision and its justification, which a change feed does not carry.
  • Stream Processing — When the question is aggregate behaviour over time rather than the defensibility of one specific decision.

Used in 6 design problems

  • Design a Payment System (Wallet / Ledger) — A double-entry ledger is the audit trail: the balance is derived from immutable entries rather than stored, so any figure can be re-proved from the entries that produced it.
  • Design an Ad Click Aggregator — Raw events are kept in object storage and a nightly batch recompute is the authority, so the fast streaming number is always reconcilable against a durable record of what actually arrived.
  • Design a Financial Crime Alert Investigation System — An examiner can ask years later why one alert was cleared, so the trace pins prompt, model snapshot, policy version and evidence hashes — everything whose change would change the answer.
  • Design a Sanctions Screening System — Every screening decision records the list version it ran against, since 'we did not know' is only defensible with the evidence attached.
  • Design a Human Review Queue — A reviewer's decision is written as an immutable event with its reason and the lease that authorised it, and a correction references the original rather than overwriting it.
  • Design a Business Verification Pipeline — Sources disagree routinely, so the record keeps what each one said and which precedence rule decided between them, rather than silently keeping the winner.
