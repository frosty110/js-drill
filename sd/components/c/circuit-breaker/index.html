Circuit Breaker

Tripping a dependency call to fail fast after repeated failures, then probing periodically to see whether it has recovered.

Reach for it when

  • A downstream dependency can fail or slow down and you must not go down with it
  • The prompt asks how the system degrades when one service is unavailable
  • Retries against a failing dependency would make its recovery harder

Don't reach for it when

  • The dependency is genuinely required and there is no meaningful fallback — failing fast just fails faster
  • Failures are per-request and independent rather than indicative of dependency health

What it costs you

  • Thresholds and probe intervals are tuning parameters with no obviously right value
  • A fallback path that is rarely exercised is a fallback path that does not work
  • State is usually per-process, so behaviour differs across the fleet

How it breaks

  • Tripping on a transient blip and cutting off a healthy dependency
  • Half-open probes stampeding a recovering service and re-tripping it immediately
  • Breaker around a non-critical call taking down the critical path because the fallback was never tested

Instead, consider

  • Retry with Backoff & Jitter — Retries handle transient failures; a breaker handles sustained ones. You need both, in that order.
  • Bulkhead Isolation — Isolate the resources a bad dependency can consume instead of detecting it after the fact.
