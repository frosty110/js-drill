Distributed Lock

A lease held in a shared store that grants one holder the right to act on a resource for a bounded time.

Reach for it when

  • A critical section spans processes and double execution would corrupt state
  • A cheap mutual exclusion is needed for a short operation
  • You need to serialise access to something that has no transaction of its own

Don't reach for it when

  • The operation could be made idempotent instead — that is almost always the better answer
  • The lock would be held across a long or user-driven operation
  • You are using it as a correctness guarantee without fencing tokens

What it costs you

  • Every acquisition is a network round trip on the critical path
  • TTL choice is a genuine tradeoff: too short breaks long work, too long stalls after a crash
  • The lock store becomes a hard dependency and a bottleneck

How it breaks

  • Holder pauses (GC, VM freeze) past its TTL while still believing it holds the lock
  • No fencing token, so a stale holder's write is accepted after the lock moved on
  • Deadlock or livelock from multiple locks acquired in inconsistent order
  • Lock-store failover losing lock state entirely and admitting two holders

Instead, consider

  • Idempotency — Make the double execution harmless instead of preventing it. Fewer moving parts, no TTL to tune.
  • Message Ordering — Serialise by routing to one partition owner rather than by contending on a lock.
