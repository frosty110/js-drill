DNS & Anycast Routing

Steering users to a region or PoP before a connection is ever made, using DNS answers or a single IP announced from many locations.

Reach for it when

  • The prompt is global and the first requirement is 'users hit the nearest region'
  • You need region-level failover, not server-level
  • Traffic must be split by geography, weight or latency at the entry point

Don't reach for it when

  • Fast failover is required — resolver and client TTL caching means DNS changes take minutes to hours to propagate
  • You are steering individual requests; by the time DNS answered, the request already committed to an address

What it costs you

  • TTL is a tradeoff you cannot escape: short TTLs cost query volume, long TTLs cost failover time
  • Clients and resolvers routinely ignore your TTL anyway
  • Anycast requires network-layer control most teams do not have in-house

How it breaks

  • Stale cached answers keep sending users to a region you just drained
  • Anycast route flaps mid-connection can break long-lived TCP sessions
  • GeoDNS resolves by RESOLVER location, not user location — a centralised corporate resolver mis-steers a whole company

Instead, consider

  • Load Balancer — Use DNS to pick the region, an LB to pick the server. They compose; they do not compete.
  • CDN — A CDN already does global steering as part of its product — usually the cheaper answer for static content.
