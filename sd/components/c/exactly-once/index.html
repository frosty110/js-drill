Exactly-Once (Effectively-Once)

Making duplicate delivery invisible in the result, by pairing at-least-once transport with idempotent or transactional application.

Reach for it when

  • The prompt counts money, inventory or billable events where a double-apply is a real defect
  • Aggregates must be exact rather than approximately right
  • You control the sink well enough to commit offset and effect together

Don't reach for it when

  • The sink is a third party you cannot make transactional
  • Approximate results are acceptable — you are buying an expensive guarantee for nothing

What it costs you

  • Deduplication state per key, which must be kept for at least the retry window
  • Transactional writes are slower and constrain which sinks you may use
  • It is only exactly-once within the boundary you control; the edges remain at-least-once

How it breaks

  • Dedup window expiring before a delayed retry arrives, admitting the duplicate you designed against
  • Dedup store and sink not committed atomically, so a crash between them re-applies
  • Believing the broker's exactly-once flag covers your external side effects — it does not

Instead, consider

  • Idempotency — Usually the cheaper route: make the operation safe to repeat and stop trying to prevent repeats.
  • At-Least-Once Delivery — If duplicates are harmless, do not pay for this.

Used in 2 design problems

  • Design a Payment System (Wallet / Ledger) — A transfer must debit and credit exactly once; a duplicate here is money moved twice, so the ledger write and the dedup record commit together.
  • Design an Ad Click Aggregator — Click counts must not double under retry, which is why the aggregation sink and the offset commit are made atomic with each other.
