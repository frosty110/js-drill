Geospatial Index

Mapping 2-D coordinates onto a 1-D key that preserves locality — geohash, quadtree or S2 — so 'what is near me' becomes a range scan.

Indexes & Search signature mechanism 2 problems

Reach for it when

  • The prompt asks for nearby drivers, restaurants, stores or users
  • Location updates are frequent and proximity queries must be fast
  • A naive distance calculation over every row would be a full scan

Don't reach for it when

  • Locations are static and few
  • Queries are by region name or administrative boundary rather than by radius
  • Exact distance ordering matters more than fast candidate retrieval

What it costs you

  • Precision level is a tradeoff between cell size and the number of cells to scan
  • Boundary handling requires querying neighbouring cells, not just the containing one
  • High-frequency location updates are a write-heavy workload of their own

How it breaks

  • Edge cases at cell boundaries missing a genuinely nearby result
  • Dense urban cells holding orders of magnitude more entities than rural ones
  • Fixed precision performing badly in both dense and sparse regions simultaneously
  • Write amplification from updating the index on every location ping

Instead, consider

  • Sharding — Geo cells make a natural partition key, but they inherit population skew — plan for it.
  • Cache — Popular areas are queried repeatedly; a short-TTL cache absorbs most of the read load.

Used in 2 design problems

  • Design a Ride-Sharing Service — Indexes driver locations by cell, so 'nearby available drivers' is a range scan rather than a distance calculation across the fleet.
  • Design a Proximity Service — The core structure: businesses indexed by geohash or S2 cell, with neighbouring cells scanned to catch results just over a boundary.
