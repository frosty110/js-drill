Ranking

Ordering a candidate set by a score — recency, affinity, engagement, relevance — usually as a cheap retrieval stage followed by an expensive scoring stage.

Indexes & Search signature mechanism 6 problems

Reach for it when

  • There are far more matching items than can be shown, and order is the product
  • The prompt mentions feeds, recommendations, search results or 'what the user sees first'
  • Simple chronological ordering demonstrably under-serves users

Don't reach for it when

  • Chronological is the expected contract and reordering would violate user trust
  • The candidate set is small enough that order barely matters
  • You cannot measure whether the ranking is working

What it costs you

  • A feature pipeline, and features are the expensive, high-maintenance part
  • Scoring latency forces a multi-stage architecture: cheap retrieval, expensive rank on the top-K
  • Training and serving must agree, or you get skew that is very hard to debug

How it breaks

  • Training/serving skew from features computed differently in the two paths
  • Feedback loops where the ranker promotes what it already promoted
  • Cold start for new users and new items with no signal to rank on
  • Score computed over a stale candidate set, ranking items that no longer exist

Instead, consider

  • Feature Store / Serving — The infrastructure ranking depends on — where the signals come from at request time.
  • Pre-Aggregation & Rollups — Precomputed counters make a serviceable ranking signal without a model.

Used in 6 design problems

  • Design a News Feed — Orders the assembled timeline by recency, affinity and engagement rather than pure chronology — the second half of the feed problem after fan-out.
  • Design a RAG / Semantic Search System — Reranks vector-retrieved candidates before they reach the model, because approximate-nearest-neighbour recall is not the same thing as relevance.
  • Design a Search Engine — Scores matching documents so the top-K is worth reading. Retrieval finds candidates; ranking is what makes the result page useful.
  • Design a Short-Form Video Feed — Drives the entire product. The feed is not a follow graph but a ranked stream, so the ranker IS the short-form video experience.
  • Design a Content Moderation Pipeline — Prioritises the review queue so the most likely violations and the highest-reach content reach a human moderator first.
  • Design a Sanctions Screening System — Scoring the surviving candidates is where date of birth, nationality and identifiers separate a genuine hit from a common surname, and it produces three bands rather than a boolean.
