Rate Limiting

Capping how many requests a caller may make in a window, to protect capacity and enforce fairness.

Reach for it when

  • A public API where one caller could consume everyone's capacity
  • The prompt mentions abuse, scraping, brute force or tiered plans
  • You call a third party with its own quota you must not exceed

Don't reach for it when

  • The bottleneck is internal and unbounded work, not caller volume — that is load shedding or backpressure
  • Legitimate bursts are normal and a hard cap would break the product

What it costs you

  • Distributed counters mean either shared state on the hot path or approximate limits
  • Limit values are a product decision that will be wrong at first
  • Every rejected request still costs you the work of rejecting it

How it breaks

  • Fixed-window boundary allowing double the limit across the boundary instant
  • Limiter store outage forcing a fail-open (unprotected) or fail-closed (outage) choice you never made explicitly
  • Limiting by IP and blocking an entire NAT'd office or mobile carrier
  • No Retry-After, so well-behaved clients retry immediately and amplify the overload

Instead, consider

  • Backpressure — Rate limiting protects you from callers; backpressure tells callers to slow down because you are full.
  • Load Shedding — When you must drop work by priority under overload rather than by per-caller quota.

Used in 5 design problems

  • Design a Web Crawler — Per-domain politeness. The crawler caps its own request rate per host — a limiter pointed at itself rather than at incoming callers.
  • Design a Distributed Rate Limiter — The problem itself. Every decision here is about where the counter lives and how exact it needs to be across a fleet of gateways.
  • Design a Webhook Delivery System — Delivery attempts per receiver endpoint are capped, so retries toward a struggling customer do not turn into an attack on them.
  • Design a Content Moderation Pipeline — Caps per-uploader submission rate — the cheapest defence against bulk spam, applied before any model spends compute on the content.
  • Design an Authentication & Session Service — Login attempts per account and per IP, the primary defence against credential stuffing and brute force.
