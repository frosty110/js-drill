Time-Series Store

A store specialised for append-only timestamped measurements, with automatic downsampling, retention and time-range queries.

Reach for it when

  • The prompt is metrics, telemetry, IoT readings, or click events keyed by time
  • Old data becomes less valuable at a predictable rate and can be rolled up
  • Queries are almost always 'this series, this window, this aggregate'

Don't reach for it when

  • Records get updated after they are written — TSDBs assume append-only
  • High-cardinality dimensions per point; label cardinality is the classic way to melt one
  • You need transactional guarantees or joins

What it costs you

  • Cardinality is the budget, and it is easy to blow up accidentally
  • Retention and rollup policy is a product decision you must make early
  • Another store class to operate and to back up

How it breaks

  • Cardinality explosion from a label like user_id or request_id
  • Queries spanning long windows at full resolution overwhelming memory
  • Late-arriving points landing after a window was already rolled up

Instead, consider

  • Stream Processing — If the aggregate matters more than the raw points, compute it in-flight and never store them.
  • Wide-Column Store — A generic partition-by-entity, cluster-by-time table when you need control over the layout.

Used in 2 design problems

  • Design an Ad Click Aggregator — Stores per-window click counts with retention and rollup handled by the engine rather than by a job you maintain.
  • Design an Observability Platform — The natural home for metrics — downsampling and retention are built in, and label cardinality becomes the budget to manage.
