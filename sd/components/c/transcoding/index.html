Transcoding Pipeline

Converting uploaded media into the ladder of formats, resolutions and bitrates that clients actually stream.

Reach for it when

  • Users upload video or audio in arbitrary formats
  • Playback must adapt to device capability and network conditions
  • The prompt mentions streaming, adaptive bitrate or thumbnails

Don't reach for it when

  • Media is already in a single known format every client supports
  • Volume is low enough that an off-the-shelf managed service is obviously right

What it costs you

  • CPU or GPU capacity that dwarfs the rest of the system
  • Storage multiplies by the number of renditions you generate
  • Latency between upload and availability is now a product decision

How it breaks

  • Queue backing up during an upload spike, so publish latency grows without bound
  • A single enormous file monopolising workers and starving everything behind it
  • Partial ladder published, so some clients get a resolution that does not exist
  • Retrying a half-finished job and paying full cost again with no checkpointing

Instead, consider

  • Chunking — Segmenting first makes transcoding parallel and restartable per segment.
  • CDN — Transcoding makes the renditions; the CDN is what makes serving them affordable.

Used in 3 design problems

  • Design a Video Streaming Service — Converts each upload into the full bitrate ladder, so players can adapt to changing network conditions mid-playback.
  • Design a Live Streaming Service — Must run in near-real-time against live ingest, which is exactly what makes it harder here than in the on-demand case.
  • Design a Short-Form Video Feed — Produces the short vertical renditions and thumbnails, at a volume driven by upload rate rather than by watch time.
