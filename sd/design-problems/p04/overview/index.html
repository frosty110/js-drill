Video Streaming Service system map

System map study sheet

What this sheet shows

Say ~1M uploads/day at ~10 min each; transcoding into ~5 renditions makes roughly 1 GB stored per upload, so ~1 PB/day of new storage — large but linear, and object storage handles it.

Trace the flow

  1. High-level architecture — The control plane manages metadata and jobs; the data plane moves video objects and segments through storage and CDNs.
  2. Distributed transcoding DAG — One uploaded master fans into parallel codec and resolution jobs, then a manifest is published only after required segments exist.
  3. Adaptive bitrate playback loop — The player measures throughput and buffer health before selecting the next segment quality from the manifest.
  4. Hot delivery and cold storage tiers — Popular segments stay replicated at the edge; rarely watched masters and renditions migrate to cheaper storage.

What this belongs to

A YouTube/Netflix-style platform where creators upload video, the system transcodes it into multiple resolutions/bitrates chunked into short segments, and viewers stream it with adaptive bitrate over a CDN. The signature challenges are the asynchronous transcoding pipeline and pushing the enormous read bandwidth to the edge.
