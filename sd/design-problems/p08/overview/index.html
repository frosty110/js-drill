Trace the flow

  1. High-level architecture — Enforce limits at the edge, but coordinate counters atomically so a fleet shares one logical quota.
  2. Atomic shared counter update — Every gateway executes the increment, expiry, and limit check as one server-side operation to eliminate read-modify-write races.
  3. Lease local tokens to reduce store traffic — The global store allocates small token batches to gateways; requests spend locally, trading exactness for lower latency.
  4. Explicit fail-open or fail-closed policy — When the counter store is unavailable, low-risk traffic may fail open while sensitive endpoints fail closed; clients receive actionable throttling metadata.

What this belongs to

A shared service (or middleware) that caps how many requests a client may make per time window across a fleet of servers. The signature challenge is the DISTRIBUTED part: a counter shared across nodes, the read-modify-write race, and fixing it atomically — plus choosing an algorithm (token bucket vs sliding window) and returning a correct 429 + Retry-After.
