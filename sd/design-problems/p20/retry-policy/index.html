Retry, backoff and dead-lettering

Lifecycle study sheet

What this sheet shows

Classify first, back off with jitter, cap the attempts, and dead-letter what remains so a poisoned job stops consuming fleet capacity.

Show that not every failure deserves a retry, and that retries need a bound.

Trace the flow

  1. Classify the failure — Transient faults retry; malformed input never will succeed.
  2. Back off exponentially — Give a struggling dependency room to recover.
  3. Add jitter — Prevent every retry synchronizing into a thundering herd.
  4. Cap the attempts — Bound the budget rather than retrying forever.
  5. Dead-letter and surface — Exhausted jobs become visible with their failure history.

What this belongs to

Run millions of scheduled and on-demand jobs across a worker fleet, on time and without accidental duplicates. The signature challenge is that at-least-once execution is the only guarantee a distributed system can actually offer, so correctness has to come from idempotent jobs rather than from perfect delivery.
