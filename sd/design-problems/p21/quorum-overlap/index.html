Why R + W > N works

Mechanism study sheet

What this sheet shows

If writes land on W replicas and reads consult R, and R + W exceeds N, the sets must share a node — so a read always sees at least one copy of the newest acknowledged write.

Reduce the quorum rule to the pigeonhole argument a candidate can redraw from memory.

Trace the flow

  1. Write to W replicas — The write is acknowledged once W members confirm.
  2. Read from R replicas — The coordinator gathers R responses and compares versions.
  3. Force an overlap — R + W > N guarantees at least one replica appears in both sets.
  4. Resolve by version — The newest version wins, and read repair pushes it to the stale replicas.

What this belongs to

Build a Dynamo/Cassandra-style durable key-value store: consistent hashing for placement, quorum replication for tunable consistency, and anti-entropy to repair the divergence that leaderless writes inevitably create.
