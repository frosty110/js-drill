Immutable segments, refresh and merge

Lifecycle study sheet

What this sheet shows

Freshness comes from sealing a small in-memory segment, not from mutating the index — and the cost is a segment count that merging has to keep bounded.

Show where near-real-time search comes from and what it costs.

Trace the flow

  1. Buffer in memory — New documents accumulate without touching existing segments.
  2. Refresh to seal — The buffer becomes a small immutable segment and is searchable.
  3. Log for durability — A translog makes the document durable before it is visible.
  4. Delete by marking — An update is a deletion marker plus a reinsert, exactly like an LSM tree.
  5. Merge in the background — Small segments combine, reclaiming deleted space and bounding query cost.

What this belongs to

Serve full-text queries over billions of documents in under a second. The signature challenge is that the inverted index inverts the obvious data layout — you store term to documents rather than document to terms — and everything else follows from making that index shardable, rankable and refreshable.
