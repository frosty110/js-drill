The latency target is an architectural fork

Comparison study sheet

What this sheet shows

Cacheable files scale to millions for almost nothing; sub-second means a connection per viewer and cost that scales linearly with audience.

Force the latency decision before anything else is designed.

Trace the flow

  1. State the target — Ten seconds, three seconds and half a second are three different systems.
  2. Segmented HTTP — Fully cacheable, marginal cost per viewer near zero.
  3. Low-latency HLS — Partial segments over chunked transfer, still mostly cacheable.
  4. WebRTC — Genuine interactivity through forwarding units, linear cost.
  5. Consider a hybrid — Interactive few on WebRTC, passive many on HLS.

What this belongs to

Broadcast one creator's video to millions of concurrent viewers with only seconds of delay, plus a live chat that fans out to the same audience. The signature challenge is that latency and scale pull in opposite directions — the CDN that makes a million viewers affordable is the same thing that adds the delay interactivity can't tolerate.
