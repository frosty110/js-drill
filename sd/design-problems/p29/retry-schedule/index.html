Backoff, jitter and the retry budget

Lifecycle study sheet

What this sheet shows

Retrying a 401 never succeeds. Retrying without jitter re-kills a recovering endpoint as every subscriber fires at once.

Show that response class decides whether a retry makes sense at all.

Trace the flow

  1. Classify the response — Only 5xx, timeouts and 429 are worth retrying.
  2. Honour Retry-After — A 429 tells you exactly when to come back.
  3. Back off exponentially — Ten seconds to six hours across the budget.
  4. Jitter every interval — Otherwise all subscribers retry simultaneously.
  5. Dead-letter and expose — Exhausted deliveries become a replayable customer-visible record.

What this belongs to

Deliver events to thousands of customer-controlled HTTP endpoints reliably, when those endpoints are slow, down, or wrong. The signature challenge is that the receiver is outside your control, so the design is almost entirely about failure isolation and retry discipline.
